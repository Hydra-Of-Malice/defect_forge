# What Wins Large AI Hackathons, 2024–2026 (Virtual, Global, Sponsor-Run, Cerebral Valley-Run), Turned Into Guidance for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon

Context for the report writer: the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon is run with Cerebral Valley. Per one secondary listing it is a 10-day virtual event (Oct 26 – Nov 4, 2026) with applications screened on "clarity and feasibility of the proposed project" and "demonstrated technical skills", and a $1M cash pool — [Autobound summary of Meta call for applications](https://www.autobound.ai/companies/meta/news/call-for-applications-meta-global-ai-developer-hackathon-5c29e0b9). Its closest structural analogs are Cerebral Valley's "Built with Opus 4.6" and "Built with Opus 4.7" virtual Claude Code hackathons (application-gated, about 500 builders, async Stage 1 judging, a top-6 live final). They are the most heavily weighted evidence below.

## Q1. Who won comparable mega-hackathons in 2024–2026, and why?

### Takeaway
In 2025–2026 the big virtual sponsor hackathons were won mostly by **domain experts who built vertical agents for a painful, specific workflow they knew personally**: permits, clinical training, road appraisal, board-level electronics repair, factory maintenance. The other winning group was **accessibility and real-world perception apps** that use vision, video, or voice in a way only the newest model makes possible. "Creative" side prizes went to **real-time, multimodal, playful instruments**, not to chatbots. Repeatedly, winners showed **quantified proof** (eval scores, tests, latency, time saved) and **early traction** (pilots, a company already in place).

### Inferences
- The two Cerebral Valley virtual events most like Meta's (500-builder, application-gated, async-judged) produced the same winner archetype twice. The builder is a **domain insider**. The project is a **multi-step agent that does a real professional's job end-to-end** on real artifacts (blueprints, schematics, dashcam video, clinical guidelines). The proof is a concrete artifact or metric (an eval percentage, test count, or time saved), plus a named real user group or pilot.
- Side prizes reliably reward a different archetype: **real-time, sensory, playful** projects (Conductr, Virtual Puppet Theater, Outdraw AI). This maps directly onto Meta's **Indie** track (creativity, technical ambition, it works). The vertical-agent archetype maps onto the **Startup** track (technical execution, holds up in production).
- Accessibility and perception (vision or video for blind users, ALS, cognitive disability) has won at Google events every year from 2024 to 2026, and Vite Vere won twice. It is a proven, judge-friendly category, especially where vision or segmentation is the core of the project. Meta's SAM 3.1 and Muse Image make it a natural fit (inference).
- Video understanding has won at Meta's own event (Llama CCTV Operator), at Anthropic's (TARA), and at Google's (Sixth Sense, PenguinAgent). Natural-language-defined video events with zero fine-tuning look like a strong pattern to build on with SAM 3.1 (inference).

## Q2. What do judges and organizers publicly say they reward and penalize?

### Takeaway
Published rubrics from the closest analogs converge. **Does it work and hold up live** carries the most weight. Next come **real-world impact for a named beneficiary** and **non-obvious use of the sponsor's specific new capabilities** ("beyond basic integration", "not just faked for the demo"). Then comes **depth**: iteration past the first idea and engineering craft. Chat wrappers are explicitly called out as what organizers do not want.

### Cited Findings
- **Cerebral Valley / Anthropic "Built with Opus 4.6" official rubric (Stage 1):** **Demo 30%**: "works, holds up live, compelling to watch". **Impact 25%**: real-world potential, who benefits, fit with a problem statement. **Opus 4.6 Use 25%**: creativity, "going beyond basic integration", "surfacing surprising capabilities". **Depth & Execution 20%**: "iteration beyond the first idea", engineering soundness, "evidence of real craft". Problem tracks: "Build a Tool That Should Exist", "Break the Barriers", "Amplify Human Judgment". Side prizes: "Most Creative Exploration" (an unexpected capability or use case) and "Keep Thinking" (iterated past the obvious first idea). Source is a copy of the rules uploaded by the PostVisit.ai team; the official page wasn't retrieved — [rules copy on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md)
- The PostVisit team's own strategy notes (same file) flagged "Opus 4.6 use" and "depth/execution" as their weak spots and planned to showcase specific model features: 1M context, extended thinking, prompt caching, 128K output. Contestants treated **explicit demonstration of the sponsor's headline features** as score-moving — [rules copy on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md)
- **Gemini 3 Hackathon (Devpost):** **Technical Execution 40%** (quality app development, leverages Gemini 3, code good and functional), **Innovation / Wow Factor 30%**, **Potential Impact 20%**, **Presentation / Demo 10%**. It requires a ~200-word description of **which Gemini 3 features were used and how they are central**. The challenge is "strictly for new applications" and wants "more than just another chat interface" — [Orendra summary of rules](https://orendra.com/blog/the-gemini-3-hackathon-2026-a-deep-dive-into-deadlines-rules-and-developer-opportunities/); [CompeteHub](https://www.competehub.dev/en/competitions/devpost27555); [official rules](https://gemini3.devpost.com/rules)
- **Kaggle "Vibe Code with Gemini 3 Pro" (Google DeepMind):** **Impact 40%** ("solve a real-world problem?"), **Technical Depth & Execution 30%** ("Does the application work?", use of multimodality, reasoning, and context, and tech that is "not just faked for the demo"), **Creativity 20%** (originality and **how many Gemini 3 capabilities are used and how well they work together**), **Presentation 10%** (engaging, well-produced, "viral potential") — [gist copy of rules](https://gist.github.com/bigsnarfdude/19be893b58c844cb43b2d30d07f31af0); [Kaggle overview](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gemini-3/overview)
- Judge rationales at the 2024 Gemini competition: Jayu as "a glimpse of AI working for people in daily life"; Vite Vere for promoting self-reliance; Outdraw AI for making AI an opponent rather than a collaborator; Prospera for practical business value — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/announcing-the-winners-of-the-gemini-api-developer-competition/)
- Gemma 4 Good judge language (Aug 2026): "closed-loop data engine", "user empathy", "offline fallback loop", "auditable reasoning trails", "deterministic templates … avoid hallucination", quantified word-error-rate gains, "memory-aware model routing and disabling thinking mode for latency" — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/)
- The lablab.ai guide (lablab runs many large virtual AI hackathons) scores four dimensions. **Presentation**: clarity over production value. **Business value**: a specific target user, rough market size, a revenue model, why it needs AI. **Application of technology**: a working demo, real repo, deployed URL, "AI that does more than wrap a chatbot around a form". **Originality**: a fresh angle "only feasible with current models" — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)
- Other rubrics: Microsoft AI Agents Hackathon 2025 used innovation, impact, technical usability, and category alignment — [Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/AI_Agents_Hackathon/). AAAI 2025 used equally weighted criteria that teams must address in the video — [AAAI rules](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/AAAI-2025-Hackathon-Rules.pdf)
- Critique relevant to a "production" rubric: hackathon demos are "only theoretical proof". They don't show the software still working 6 months later or handling real-world cases, and auditability was not prioritized — Dexter Hadley in [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260425-anthropic-hackathon/)
- Startup angle at sponsor events: the Gemini 3 grand prize included an AI Futures Fund interview — [Devpost updates](https://gemini3.devpost.com/updates). Opus 4.7 winners cited pilots (MedKit) and existing companies (Wrench Board via RepairMind; ARIA folding into an IIoT platform) — [Claude winners page](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon)

### Inferences
- For Meta's **Indie** track ("creativity, technical ambition, works"), the CV/Anthropic rubric suggests you need a working, compelling demo plus a **"surprising capability"** moment that shows something the Muse or SAM models can do that wasn't possible before.
- For Meta's **Startup** track ("technical execution, holds up in production"), Hadley's critique and the Gemma 4 judges' praise point the same way. Show production signals inside the demo: an **eval harness with a number**, error handling or fallback paths, latency figures, audit trails or citations, and a real data source. Add a named pilot or user if you can.
- Using several of the sponsor's models together (e.g. Muse Spark reasoning + SAM 3.1 segmentation + Muse Image generation) is explicitly rewarded in Google's rubric ("how many … capabilities are used and how well they work together"). It is likely rewarded by Meta's judges too as the "use of sponsor models" signal, but that is an inference.

## Q3. How do mega-hackathons with 10k+ entrants screen submissions? (Video, README, live link, the first 30 seconds)

### Takeaway
Screening is **video-first and time-boxed**. Judges watch at most about 3 minutes, often score asynchronously and independently against a standardized rubric, and use the write-up, repo, and live link only to **verify** what the video claims. Cerebral Valley's format is **async Stage 1, then a top 6 showing pre-recorded 3-minute demos live, then deliberation**. The opening 30 seconds have to make the problem and the "aha" obvious.

### Cited Findings
- **CV / Anthropic Opus 4.6 process:** Stage 1 async judging over about 2 days (Feb 16–17), in which **six Anthropic judges evaluate each project independently** with standardized criteria. Top 6 announced Feb 18. In Stage 2 the **top 6 present pre-recorded 3-minute demos** and judges deliberate on the top 3. Requirements: **video ≤3 min** (YouTube or Loom), **public GitHub repo**, a **100–200-word summary**, everything shown must be **open source**, built from scratch during the event, teams of at most 2 — [rules copy on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md). Funnel: 13,000+ applicants → 500 selected → 227 projects → 6 finalists → 3 winners — [Cerebral Valley on X](https://x.com/cerebral_valley/status/2026066211482857844)
- **Kaggle Gemini 3:** "The video demo is the primary lens for judging, with the Writeup and app serving as verification and technical depth." — [gist copy of rules](https://gist.github.com/bigsnarfdude/19be893b58c844cb43b2d30d07f31af0)
- **Gemini 3 Devpost:** ~3-minute video, and judges may not watch beyond 3 minutes. It requires a **public project link to a working product that doesn't require a login or paywall**, plus a public repo — [Orendra summary](https://orendra.com/blog/the-gemini-3-hackathon-2026-a-deep-dive-into-deadlines-rules-and-developer-opportunities/). With ~35K participants, judging took about 7 weeks — [Devpost updates](https://gemini3.devpost.com/updates)
- Video cutoffs are enforced: "judges will be instructed to stop watching your video after the first three minutes" — [UniHack](https://unihack.notion.site/How-to-submit-your-project-2e05dc7357fb4d3ba3e23f51207f996a); "anything beyond the 3 minute mark … will not be considered" — [ACM UTSA](https://acmutsa.notion.site/Submitting-Your-Project-to-Devpost-9e7014a39d5f481d8439f63f04a78300)
- A **pass/fail Stage One** is a common triage step: does the project "reasonably fit the theme and reasonably apply the required APIs/SDKs"? Only passing entries get scored on the full rubric — [Okta dev forum](https://devforum.okta.com/t/what-are-the-judging-criteria-for-the-hackathon/15842)
- The first-30-seconds bar: Nebius x NVIDIA Global AI Hackathon judges said the aha moment should come **within ~2 minutes into the project and ~30 seconds into the demo** (from a search snippet; page returned 403) — [Nebius x NVIDIA Devpost updates](https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/updates). Similarly, "a judge should grasp the value from the opening screen within 30 seconds" — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)
- LlamaCon used **two judging rounds to reach a top 6** — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llamacon-hackathon/). The Meta hackathon's application screen weighed "clarity and feasibility of the proposed project" and "demonstrated technical skills" — [Autobound summary](https://www.autobound.ai/companies/meta/news/call-for-applications-meta-global-ai-developer-hackathon-5c29e0b9)
- What else gets checked: a deployed, accessible URL; a GitHub repo with commits spread across the event (not one final push); a tight "golden path" with every bug on the demo path fixed — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)

### Inferences
- Meta's window is Nov 4–8 for Stage 1 (4 days) with finals on Nov 9, the same shape as CV's Opus format (async Stage 1, then a live final with pre-recorded demos). At Opus 4.6 scale (227 projects, 6 judges, about 11 hours of video each) judges watched everything. If Meta gets thousands of submissions, a **pass/fail triage on the video's first minute plus the summary** is very likely, followed by fuller rubric scoring of a shortlist. That is inference from the analogs, not a published Meta process.
- So: the **first 20–30 seconds of the video** and the **100–200-word summary** effectively are the Stage 1 submission. The repo and live link are credibility checks, and a broken link or empty README is an easy cut.
- Because finalists **replay their pre-recorded 3-minute demo** in the live final, the same video must also work in front of a room. Optimize for both: a fast hook and a clear narrative.

### Gaps
- Meta's exact Stage 1 mechanics (number of judges, whether all videos are watched, any pass/fail gate) weren't found.
- I found no hard data on what share of entries are cut at triage at Devpost-scale events.

## Q4. Which idea categories were oversaturated and penalized by 2026, and which were fresh?

### Takeaway
Saturated: **generic chat interfaces and wrappers** (organizers say so explicitly), **shallow RAG over documents**, and **generic "AI tutor / AI assistant for X"**. Fresh and rewarded in 2025–2026: **vertical agents that do a professional's multi-step job on real artifacts**; **video and vision understanding with real-world inputs** (dashcam, CCTV, webcam, camera feeds); **real-time multimodal and creative instruments**; **physical-world, edge, and robotics** (VLA, Raspberry Pi, sensors); and **accessibility**. Education still wins, but only when it is **inverted or simulation-based**, not "ask the tutor".

### Cited Findings
- Explicit organizer anti-patterns: "Go beyond chat wrappers. Build systems that plan, execute, and self-correct—with real-time vision, audio, and reasoning." — [Devpost / Google DeepMind on X](https://x.com/devpost/status/2001779137598623900); they want "more than just another chat interface" — [Orendra summary](https://orendra.com/blog/the-gemini-3-hackathon-2026-a-deep-dive-into-deadlines-rules-and-developer-opportunities/); "going beyond basic integration" — [Opus 4.6 rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md); "AI that does more than wrap a chatbot around a form" — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)
- Advice blogs (low-quality sources; directional only) say judges are tired of "ChatGPT wrappers" and "AI chatbot for X", and that AI is now a baseline rather than a differentiator — [Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/hackathon-ideas-that-won-2026-judges-actually-pick-examples/); [EHGA](https://ehga.org/win-hackathons-in-2026-vibe-coding-llm-agents-strategy)
- **RAG is not dead when it is grounded in a niche and paired with other modalities:** PenguinAgent (video + telemetry + RAG over ornithology papers) placed 3rd, and PreVillage's "self-healing RAG loop" won a prize — [Google blog, Gemma 4 Good](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/)
- **Fresh category evidence: vertical agents on real professional artifacts**: CrossBeam (blueprints and correction letters), TARA (dashcam to NPV), PostVisit (visit transcripts) — [Claude blog 4.6](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon); Wrench Board (schematics and boardviews), ARIA (manufacturer PDFs plus live signals) — [Claude 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon); Geo-ML (400-page geology reports to 3D models), OrgLens (Jira and GitHub knowledge graph) — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llamacon-hackathon/)
- **Fresh: video and vision on live or real-world feeds**: Llama CCTV Operator (natural-language-defined events, no fine-tuning) — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llamacon-hackathon/); Sixth Sense for Security Guards (16 cameras), Gemma Vision (chest-mounted camera) — [Google blog, Gemma 3n](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/developers-changing-lives-with-gemma-3n/); Jayu (screen understanding plus UI control) — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/announcing-the-winners-of-the-gemini-api-developer-competition/)
- **Fresh: real-time multimodal and creative**: Conductr (15 ms note generation) — [Claude blog 4.6](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon); Virtual Puppet Theater (webcam, MediaPipe, Three.js, voice) — [Claude 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon); Outdraw AI, Everies (AR) — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/announcing-the-winners-of-the-gemini-api-developer-competition/)
- **Fresh: physical-world, edge, and robotics**: GEM-4 VLA robot (1st, Aug 2026), DEMENTOR (sensor network), Acuífero·Vigía (Pi 5 flood warning) — [Google blog, Gemma 4 Good](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/); LeRobot prize — [Google blog, Gemma 3n](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/developers-changing-lives-with-gemma-3n/)
- **Education winners are non-generic**: Elisa (block IDE for kids) — [Claude blog 4.6](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon); Maieutic (autocomplete off, counter-questions, Intent-Diff), MedKit (simulated patients plus guideline-based agentic grader) — [Claude 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon); Trido (offline voice whiteboard) — [Google blog, Gemma 4 Good](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/)
- **Agents that act in the real world**: MaestrIA maps tradespeople and drafts the WhatsApp message — [Claude 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon); ARIA generates work orders — same source; Compliance Wizards emails users and offers voice follow-up — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llamacon-hackathon/)

### Inferences
- **Avoid**: "chat with your PDF/docs", a generic personal assistant or chatbot, "AI tutor that answers questions", image-caption or "describe this photo" apps, single-call text-to-image toys, and thin wrappers with a pretty UI. Each conflicts with explicit organizer language or has no recent winning analog.
- **Lean into the following for Meta's model set** (all inference, mapping proven categories onto the sponsor's unique capabilities):
  - **SAM 3.1** (promptable segmentation and tracking in images and video) fits the proven "natural-language-defined video events" pattern (Llama CCTV Operator, TARA, Sixth Sense) and accessibility perception (Gemma Vision, Viddyscribe). Example: segment-and-measure from real footage, e.g. road defects, crop disease, construction progress, or sports technique, turned into a professional deliverable.
  - **Muse Spark** (reasoning or agentic) as the planner behind a vertical agent that produces a real professional artifact (permit packet, repair diagnosis, maintenance work order), with a quantified eval against an expert.
  - **Muse Image / Muse Wonder** for real-time creative or interactive experiences in the Conductr and Puppet Theater mold (Indie track), where the model is a live collaborator or opponent (the Outdraw AI framing), not a one-shot generator.
  - **Combine 2–3 Meta models** in one closed loop. See (perception) → reason (plan) → act or generate. This hits the "how many capabilities … work together" criterion.
- Accessibility plus vision has won at Google events every year from 2024 to 2026, so it is a strong but increasingly common lane. Differentiate by targeting a specific condition and workflow, with a real user in the video.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative Devpost or Cerebral Valley data on category frequency or saturation; saturation claims rest on organizer language and blog opinion.
- I found no evidence on how judges treated "AI companion / mental health" or "AI agent for shopping/travel" entries specifically in 2026. Trippy, a travel planner, won Best Firebase in 2024, but that was a sponsor-specific category prize — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/announcing-the-winners-of-the-gemini-api-developer-competition/)

## Q5. What are the recurring failure modes of strong teams?

### Takeaway
Strong teams lose on **overscope**, **a demo that doesn't work or isn't reachable**, **a late or buried video**, **shallow sponsor-model use**, and **stopping at the first idea**. Judges and organizers have built rubric line items or whole prizes ("Keep Thinking") around each of these.

### Cited Findings
- The rubric penalizes stopping at the first idea: Depth & Execution rewards "iteration beyond the first idea", and a dedicated "Keep Thinking" prize exists for a project that "iterated past the obvious first idea" — [Opus 4.6 rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md)
- Demo reliability is the single largest weight (30%, "works, holds up live") — [Opus 4.6 rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md); Google asks whether the technology is "not just faked for the demo" — [gist copy of Kaggle rules](https://gist.github.com/bigsnarfdude/19be893b58c844cb43b2d30d07f31af0)
- Failure modes listed by lablab.ai: **pivoting after hour 12**; **building without deploying** (judges can't access the demo); an **empty repo until a final push**; **chaining five LLM calls when one would do** (latency and failure points); **building settings, auth, or onboarding before the core loop works**; **recording the video too late to re-record** — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)
- A video that buries the demo: "a 4-minute video that shows the demo clearly beats a polished 5-minute video that buries it" — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon). Anything past 3 minutes is ignored at many events — [UniHack](https://unihack.notion.site/How-to-submit-your-project-2e05dc7357fb4d3ba3e23f51207f996a)
- Overscope is named as the most common cause of a broken final demo; judges prefer a well-scoped working solution over a complex broken one (blog source, directional) — [Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/hackathon-ideas-that-won-2026-judges-actually-pick-examples/)
- Model unreliability is a real build risk: Jayu's builder spent "many hours" on prompt trial and error to get reliable instruction-following — [UCLA Samueli](https://www.samueli.ucla.edu/?p=83261)
- Rule violations disqualify: pre-existing work, non-open-source components in the demo, assets without rights — [Opus 4.6 rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md). A login or paywall on the project link breaks Gemini 3 requirements — [Orendra summary](https://orendra.com/blog/the-gemini-3-hackathon-2026-a-deep-dive-into-deadlines-rules-and-developer-opportunities/)
- Under-showcasing sponsor features: the 3rd-place PostVisit team's own self-assessment flagged model use and depth as weak points to shore up — [rules copy with strategy notes](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md)

### Inferences
- With 1–1.5 days left, the highest-probability failure is **overscope that leaves no time for the video**. The video is the Stage 1 submission, so a mediocre product with a crisp 2:30 video likely outscores a better product with a rushed one.
- An unclear story is a failure mode in its own right: winners all have a one-line "who hurts and how much" (90% permit rejection, weeks to 5 hours, 74%→81% vs a master carpenter).

### Gaps
- I found no named post-mortems from strong teams that lost at a CV mega-hackathon. The failure modes come from rubric design and organizer and guide advice, not from specific losing teams.

## Q6. Tactical best practices for a 1–1.5-day build window (scope, demo-first, video script, live URL)

### Takeaway
Pick **one golden-path workflow** with a real input and a real output artifact. Build **demo-first** with something clickable by about the midpoint, deploy a **no-login live URL** early, and record the **≤3-minute video with time to re-record**. Script the video so the problem and the aha land in the first 30 seconds, explicitly name the Meta model features used, and show one hard number.

### Cited Findings
- Scope: use existing model APIs rather than training; "Have something clickable by hour 24. If you can't, the scope is too big"; name a real user ("a doctor who wants to reduce note-taking time" beats "the healthcare industry"); fix every bug on the demo path and leave the rest — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)
- Speed is feasible with agentic coding: Elisa was built in **30 hours** with 1,500+ tests — [Claude blog 4.6](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon). MedKit parallelized the work across **four concurrent coding-agent sessions** split by subsystem — [Claude 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon). Wrench Board prototyped the UI first in a design tool, then built with one agent per domain — same source.
- Credibility via evals: MaestrIA measured 74%→81% against an expert's judgment — [Claude 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon); Gem-Care reported a 19.0% vs 32.7% word error rate — [Google blog, Gemma 4 Good](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/)
- Video structure: lablab.ai suggests 0:00–0:30 problem and why now, 0:30–2:30 live demo, then business case and team. That is calibrated for about 4 minutes, so compress for a 3-minute cap — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon). The aha should come about 30 seconds into the demo — [Nebius x NVIDIA Devpost updates (snippet)](https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/updates). The AAAI rules require teams to address each criterion in the video — [AAAI rules](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/AAAI-2025-Hackathon-Rules.pdf)
- Written summary: CV used 100–200 words — [Opus 4.6 rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md); Gemini 3 required ~200 words on **which model features are used and how they're central** — [Orendra summary](https://orendra.com/blog/the-gemini-3-hackathon-2026-a-deep-dive-into-deadlines-rules-and-developer-opportunities/)
- Live link: a working product or interactive demo with **no login or paywall** — [Orendra summary](https://orendra.com/blog/the-gemini-3-hackathon-2026-a-deep-dive-into-deadlines-rules-and-developer-opportunities/); a deployed, accessible URL and a repo with commits spread across the event — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)
- Open source and from scratch: CV required everything shown to be open source and built during the event — [Opus 4.6 rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md). Check whether Meta's rules match.
- Latency tactics judges noticed: KawanIsyarat was praised for "disabling thinking mode for latency" and memory-aware routing — [Google blog, Gemma 4 Good](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/). Avoid five chained LLM calls where one would do — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)

### Inferences
**Suggested 1–1.5-day plan, working back from the Nov 4, 9:00 PM ET deadline:**
- **Hours 0–2:** Lock one user, one painful workflow, and one input-to-output golden path. Write the 100–200-word summary and the video script *first*: a demo-first spec.
- **Hours 2–14:** Build only the golden path end-to-end with real sample data (real footage or real documents). Deploy a no-login URL by about hour 8 and keep it deployed. Cache or precompute heavy model calls so the live link can't fail. Add a visible fallback path.
- **Hours 14–20:** Add the "depth" layer. Use one more Meta model in the loop (e.g. SAM 3.1 perception feeding Muse Spark reasoning), plus a tiny eval (10–30 cases scored against an expert or ground truth) that produces one number for the video.
- **Hours 20–26:** Record the video, then re-record. Write the README: a hero GIF or screenshot, a one-line pitch, an architecture diagram naming each Meta model, how to run it, the eval result, and the live link. Polish only the demo path.
- **Final buffer of 3–4 hours or more:** Submission form, link checks in an incognito window, and confirming the video is public. Do not change code.

**A 3-minute video script that fits the CV-style rubric (Demo / Impact / Model use / Depth):**
- **0:00–0:20 hook:** Show the output artifact on screen immediately, then one sentence on who hurts and the number (e.g. "X takes Y hours; this does it in Z minutes").
- **0:20–1:50 live demo:** A real input goes in, the agent or model works with its steps visible, and a real artifact comes out. The aha lands by about 0:45.
- **1:50–2:20 "how it uses Meta's models":** A labeled architecture graphic. Call out the surprising capability (e.g. SAM 3.1 tracking across video, Muse Spark multi-step planning).
- **2:20–2:45 proof:** The eval number, latency, test count, and real-user or pilot quote. For the Startup track add production signals: error handling, cost per run, audit trail.
- **2:45–3:00:** Who adopts it next and the live link. End before 3:00 so nothing is cut.

**Track mapping:**
- **Indie:** a real-time, sensory, "AI as live collaborator or opponent" experience (the Conductr, Puppet Theater, and Outdraw AI archetype) that obviously works live.
- **Startup:** a domain-expert vertical agent producing a professional artifact from real inputs (the CrossBeam, Wrench Board, ARIA, MedKit archetype), with an eval, reliability features, and a named user or pilot.

### Gaps
- I found no public data on Meta's specific submission form fields, video cap, or open-source requirement. The team should verify these against the official rules.
- I found no judge-authored guidance specific to SAM 3.1 or Muse models, so the model-to-category mappings above are inference.
